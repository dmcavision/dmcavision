This guide helps brand teams establish a consistent first-line process for reviewing potential trademark misuse online. It does not determine whether infringement has occurred and is not a substitute for legal advice.

## Build a rights inventory

Maintain a current record for each mark:

- Owner and responsible business entity
- Registration number and jurisdiction
- Mark type, classes, and covered goods or services
- Specimen or approved logo files
- Registration and renewal dates
- Authorized licensees, distributors, and sellers

Link supporting documents to the record so reviewers can verify the right without searching across disconnected systems.

## Classify the observed activity

Describe what the online material actually does. Common review categories include suspected counterfeit goods, unauthorized mark use, misleading affiliation, impersonation, copied brand assets, and paid advertising concerns.

Do not assume that every unauthorized resale or mention is trademark infringement. The facts, jurisdiction, rights, presentation, and platform rules all affect the available response.

## Capture the listing or account

Record direct URLs, platform identifiers, seller or account names, visible location information, product claims, imagery, pricing, and capture timestamps. Preserve the page before contacting the responsible party because content may be altered or removed.

Where lawful and appropriate, keep related listings together to show a seller pattern while maintaining a clear record for each reported URL.

## Select the enforcement path

Review the platform's current reporting options. A marketplace may distinguish counterfeiting from general trademark use, while a social platform may have separate impersonation and intellectual-property forms.

Choose the route that matches both the evidence and the rights held. If copied product photography is central to the case, a separate copyright analysis may also be appropriate.

## Prepare a focused report

Provide the requested rights information, exact locations, and a concise explanation. Identify the specific mark and how it appears in the reported material. Avoid broad or conclusory statements that are not tied to the evidence.

Retain a copy of the report, reference number, response, and outcome. If the platform requests clarification, add the response to the same case history.

## Monitor patterns

Track relisting, repeated seller identifiers, reused imagery, contact details, and related storefronts. Pattern data can help prioritize significant networks and measure whether enforcement is reducing exposure.

Escalate matters involving consumer safety, organized activity, substantial commercial harm, litigation risk, or disputed rights to appropriate legal or investigative specialists.
