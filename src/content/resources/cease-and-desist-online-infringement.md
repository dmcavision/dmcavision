A cease-and-desist letter communicates a rights holder's position directly to the person or business responsible for disputed activity. It may be useful when the party is identifiable, ongoing conduct needs to stop, or a platform report alone will not address the broader issue.

## Evaluate the objective

Define the requested outcome: removal, discontinued sales, preservation of records, transfer of materials, or a licensing discussion. The facts and desired resolution should determine the tone and scope.

## Preserve evidence first

Capture the relevant pages, account details, timestamps, and rights records before making contact. Content may change once the recipient becomes aware of the concern.

## Consider risk and escalation

Direct outreach can trigger a dispute, public response, jurisdictional issue, or evidence destruction concern. High-value, contested, or potentially litigated matters should receive qualified legal review.

Keep the final communication, delivery record, response, commitments, and follow-up dates in the same case history. A letter is most effective when it forms part of a planned enforcement workflow.
