Repeated infringement may appear as relisted content, replacement accounts, mirror domains, or new listings using the same assets. Treating every event as unrelated hides the pattern and duplicates work.

## Use stable identifiers

Record platform IDs, seller details, domains, contact information, destination links, asset fingerprints, and prior case references. Account names alone may change.

## Preserve each event separately

Every URL and capture needs its own evidence and outcome. Link related cases without merging facts that apply to different parties or times.

## Define repeat thresholds

Establish when repeated activity changes priority or triggers specialist review. Thresholds can consider validated events, evasion, commercial impact, safety, and platform history.

## Present supported patterns

Where a platform accepts repeat-abuse context, provide accurate case numbers and observable links. Avoid overstating identity or coordination.

A connected case history reduces duplicate review and helps teams choose responses designed for durable results rather than temporary URL removal.
