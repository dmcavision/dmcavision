Evidence can look persuasive at first glance yet fail to explain where, when, or how the material appeared. Several common mistakes reduce its usefulness.

## Capturing too little context

A crop may show copied content while omitting the URL, account, listing ID, caption, or date. Preserve the relevant page environment before creating comparison images.

## Replacing the original

Do not overwrite the only file with annotations, compression, or resized versions. Retain the original separately and label working copies clearly.

## Using ambiguous timestamps

Record date, time, and time zone, and distinguish discovery, capture, download, submission, and later review.

## Losing the ownership connection

Every reported item should link to a reliable original-work or rights record. Similarity without ownership context is incomplete.

## Failing to document change

If a page is edited or removed, preserve earlier captures and add a new status rather than silently replacing evidence.

Consistent procedures make digital records easier for independent reviewers to understand and verify.
