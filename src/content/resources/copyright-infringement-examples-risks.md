Online copyright infringement can range from a copied product photograph to the unauthorized redistribution of an entire software package. Understanding the pattern helps rights holders select the correct evidence and response.

## Common examples

- Reposting photographs or illustrations without permission
- Uploading films, courses, music, or ebooks to unauthorized services
- Copying website text, product descriptions, or marketing assets
- Distributing unlicensed software, templates, or source code
- Using protected artwork on merchandise or marketplace listings
- Creating derivative content beyond the scope of a license

Not every unapproved use is necessarily infringement. Ownership, license terms, jurisdiction, and legal exceptions must be considered.

## Business risks

Unauthorized distribution can reduce sales, weaken exclusivity, create customer confusion, and expose users to modified or unsafe files. Inconsistent enforcement can also make it difficult to identify repeat actors and measure the true scale of exposure.

## Respond with evidence

Preserve direct URLs, timestamps, account identifiers, screenshots, and an authoritative copy of the original work. Classify the issue before reporting it: copyright, trademark, impersonation, contract, or platform policy concerns may require different routes.

A structured case record allows another reviewer to understand what happened and why a particular action was selected. High-risk or disputed matters should receive appropriate legal review.
