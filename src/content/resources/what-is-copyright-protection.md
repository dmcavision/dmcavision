Copyright protects original expression such as photographs, videos, written works, software, music, and visual designs. In many jurisdictions, protection begins when an eligible work is created and fixed in a tangible form. Registration is not always required for ownership, but it can provide important procedural and evidentiary benefits.

## What copyright covers

Copyright generally protects the expression of an idea, not the underlying idea, method, fact, or concept. The rights available to an owner commonly include reproduction, distribution, public display, public performance, and creation of derivative works, subject to local law and limitations.

Ownership should be confirmed rather than assumed. Employment agreements, commissioned work terms, assignments, licenses, and joint authorship can all affect who controls a work.

## Why online protection requires preparation

Digital files can be copied and redistributed instantly. A protection program should therefore maintain source files, publication dates, ownership records, licenses, and approved distribution channels. These records help a reviewer distinguish infringement from authorized or legally permitted use.

## A practical first step

Create a rights inventory containing the work, owner, first publication, registration details where available, license status, and reference files. Connect that inventory to an evidence and enforcement workflow so potential misuse can be reviewed consistently.

Copyright enforcement is fact-specific. Complex ownership, fair-use, jurisdiction, or counter-notice questions may require advice from qualified legal counsel.
