Copied website content can affect search visibility, customer trust, and the commercial value of original materials. Responding effectively begins with confirming what is protected and preserving both versions.

## Document the original

Retain source files, publication dates, version history, author or contractor agreements, and archived copies of the original page. Identify the entity that owns each asset.

## Capture the copied page

Save the direct URL, screenshots, page source where relevant, timestamps, domain details, and the exact elements copied. Avoid describing the entire site as infringing if only specific content is at issue.

## Investigate authorization

Review syndication, agency, affiliate, distributor, and licensing relationships. Similar content may be authorized or originate from a common source.

## Choose a response

Options may include direct contact, a platform or host notice, search removal, contractual escalation, or legal review. Track the result and monitor for relocation to another domain.

This process separates an understandable concern from a supported, actionable case.
