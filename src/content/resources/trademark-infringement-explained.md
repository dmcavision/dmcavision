Trademarks identify the source of goods or services. Online misuse may create confusion about origin, sponsorship, affiliation, or approval, but the analysis depends on the mark, use, market context, jurisdiction, and rights held.

## Build a reliable rights inventory

Record the trademark owner, registration number, jurisdiction, classes, covered goods or services, approved logo files, licensees, and renewal dates. This gives reviewers a trustworthy reference for platform reports.

## Recognize different issues

Potential concerns include counterfeit goods, confusing storefront names, unauthorized logo use, misleading advertising, impersonation, and domains designed to appear official. Unauthorized resale or descriptive mention is not automatically infringement.

## Preserve the online use

Capture direct URLs, seller and account identifiers, product descriptions, images, claims of affiliation, pricing, and timestamps. Document how the mark appears and why the use warrants review.

## Choose a supported response

Platforms often separate counterfeit, trademark, copyright, and impersonation reports. Select the route that matches the facts and provide specific registrations and locations. Retain each submission and outcome.

Matters involving contested rights, comparative advertising, nominative use, jurisdictional questions, or significant commercial harm may require legal analysis before action.
