Online evidence is volatile. A page can be edited, an account can be renamed, and a listing can disappear between discovery and review. Collecting material promptly is important, but speed alone is not enough. The record should preserve enough context for another reviewer to understand what was observed, where it appeared, and when it was captured.

## Start with a collection plan

Define the question the evidence is intended to support. Are you documenting a copied photograph, an unauthorized video, a counterfeit listing, or a network of related accounts? The answer determines which pages, identifiers, and surrounding details matter.

A collection plan also reduces unnecessary capture of personal or irrelevant data. Gather what is proportionate to the infringement review and follow applicable privacy, platform, and legal requirements.

## Record the source precisely

Save the complete URL rather than a shortened link or search-results URL. For platforms that use changing parameters, also record stable identifiers such as a listing ID, post ID, channel name, seller ID, or account handle.

Note how the page was accessed and whether login, region, device, or language settings affected the view. Two reviewers may otherwise see different content at the same address.

## Preserve visible context

Screenshots should show more than the disputed asset. Include relevant page headers, account identity, product details, captions, timestamps, and the browser address where practical. For long pages, use an ordered set of captures and document any overlap.

Video or interactive material may require screen recording, but still images and written notes can help identify key moments without requiring a reviewer to replay an entire session.

## Use reliable timestamps

Record the date, time, and time zone for each collection event. System-generated timestamps can be useful, but the case record should make clear what the timestamp represents: discovery, capture, download, submission, or later review.

If evidence is updated, retain the earlier version rather than silently replacing it. A simple version history can show how the online material changed over time.

## Preserve original files and metadata

Where downloads are permitted, retain an unedited original separately from working copies. Avoid resizing, annotating, or recompressing the only copy. Record filenames and, when appropriate to the case, cryptographic hashes that can help verify that a file has not changed.

Annotations are valuable for review, but they should be clearly labeled and linked to the underlying original.

## Maintain a clear case log

For each item, record:

- A unique evidence or case identifier
- Collector or responsible team
- Source URL and platform
- Capture date, time, and time zone
- Description of what the item shows
- Original-work reference
- Storage location and version
- Related reports, responses, and outcomes

This log connects individual screenshots and files to the broader enforcement workflow.

> Good evidence allows a reviewer who was not present during collection to reconstruct the relevant online context.

## Protect integrity and access

Limit access to people who need the material, especially when records contain personal information or sensitive business data. Use controlled storage, consistent naming, and backups appropriate to the risk and retention requirements.

Document transfers or substantive handling when chain of custody is important. The necessary level of formality depends on the purpose of the evidence and the potential dispute.

## Review before enforcement

Evidence collection and infringement analysis are separate steps. Confirm that the captured material matches the protected work or right, that the reported URL remains accurate, and that the proposed enforcement ground is supported.

For high-risk, disputed, or litigation-related matters, obtain guidance from qualified legal counsel about collection methods, preservation duties, authentication, and admissibility requirements.

A consistent evidence protocol improves the quality of takedown submissions today and preserves a reliable record for decisions that may follow later.
