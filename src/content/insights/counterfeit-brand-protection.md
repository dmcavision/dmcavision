Counterfeit activity online is rarely limited to a single listing. Sellers can reuse product photography, alter account names, move inventory between platforms, and relaunch after an enforcement action. Brands therefore need a repeatable program that connects detection, verification, evidence, reporting, and follow-up.

The objective is not simply to generate a high volume of reports. It is to submit accurate, well-supported reports against the activity that creates the greatest risk.

## Define what you are protecting

Maintain an accessible record of registered trademarks, protected logos, packaging, product photography, authorized seller information, and territory-specific rights. Enforcement teams should know which entity owns each right and where it applies.

This rights inventory helps reviewers distinguish counterfeiting from other issues such as unauthorized resale, copyright misuse, misleading affiliation, or a contractual distribution dispute. Those issues may require different reporting paths.

## Detect meaningful signals

No single indicator proves that a product is counterfeit. Useful signals gain value when reviewed together:

- Pricing that is inconsistent with the genuine product and normal promotions
- Product images copied from official brand channels
- Incorrect packaging, labels, spelling, colors, or product specifications
- Seller histories showing abrupt category changes or repeated account replacement
- Claims of authenticity that conflict with the supply chain
- Customer reviews describing quality or packaging discrepancies

Prioritize listings by reach, sales activity, consumer risk, and connection to repeat sellers. A risk-based queue allows specialists to focus on consequential activity rather than treating every mention equally.

## Preserve evidence before reporting

Capture the listing as a complete record. Include the URL, seller name and identifier, listing or product ID, price, images, description, shipping location, review history, and capture time. Where appropriate and lawful, a test purchase can provide additional evidence about the product and fulfillment chain.

Store the evidence in a consistent case structure. Marketplace content changes quickly, and an incomplete snapshot can make later review difficult.

## Choose the correct enforcement ground

Marketplace forms often separate trademark counterfeiting, trademark misuse, copyright infringement, patent complaints, and other policy violations. Select the basis supported by the evidence and the rights actually owned.

For example, copied studio photography may support a copyright report, while unauthorized use of a registered mark on non-genuine goods may support a counterfeit or trademark report. A listing can raise several issues, but each allegation should remain specific and supportable.

## Submit a precise report

Provide the registration or rights information requested by the platform, identify the exact listings, and explain the relevant issue concisely. Avoid broad statements about an entire storefront when only certain product pages have been reviewed.

Some platforms offer brand-protection portals with bulk reporting, trusted reporter programs, or serialization tools. Use those systems when available, while retaining an internal copy of the submission and platform reference number.

## Monitor outcomes and relisting

An initial removal is only one data point. Track whether the platform removed the listing, requested more information, rejected the report, or took action against the seller account. Continue monitoring distinctive images, seller identifiers, contact details, and product descriptions for relisting.

Patterns across cases can reveal coordinated seller networks. They can also show which platforms, rights, evidence types, and report formats produce reliable outcomes.

## Coordinate across the business

Brand protection works best when legal, marketplace operations, customer support, investigations, and authorized distributors share relevant information. Customer complaints may reveal counterfeit quality issues, while channel teams may confirm whether a seller is authorized.

Document decision ownership and escalation thresholds. Matters involving consumer safety, significant revenue, organized networks, or contested rights may need specialist legal or investigative support.

## Measure quality as well as volume

Useful program metrics include validated infringement rate, removal rate, response time, relisting rate, repeat-seller connections, and time from detection to evidence capture. These measures show whether activity is reducing exposure rather than simply increasing the number of reports sent.

A structured evidence and enforcement workflow gives brands a clearer view of online risk and a stronger basis for consistent action.
