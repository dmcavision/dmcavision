An enforcement workflow connects several distinct decisions. Treating them as one automated step can increase error; separating them creates clear ownership and measurable quality.

## 1. Detection

Monitoring, referrals, customer reports, or investigations identify a candidate. The signal should retain its source and confidence rather than being presented as a final conclusion.

## 2. Triage

The candidate is checked for duplication, obvious authorization, relevance, and potential harm. Priority can reflect audience, commercial activity, safety, repeat behavior, and time sensitivity.

## 3. Validation

A reviewer compares the material with authoritative rights and asset records. Ownership, licenses, territory, platform context, and possible lawful use are considered.

## 4. Evidence capture

Direct URLs, account identifiers, page context, timestamps, and relevant files are preserved. The record should allow another reviewer to understand the basis for action.

## 5. Action selection and submission

The team selects the supported route—platform, host, marketplace, search, direct outreach, or escalation—and adapts the case data to the recipient's requirements.

## 6. Outcome and monitoring

Responses, removals, rejections, restoration, and relisting return to the case record. The result informs both follow-up and future detection priorities.

The workflow is complete only when the outcome is recorded. That feedback turns individual actions into an enforcement program that can improve over time.
