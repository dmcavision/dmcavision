Brand reputation is shaped by experiences that may occur outside a company's owned channels. A counterfeit product, fake support account, misleading advertisement, or copied campaign can influence customer trust before the brand knows the activity exists.

## Reputation risk crosses disciplines

Not every harmful statement is an intellectual property issue, and not every infringement creates the same reputation risk. Legal, security, customer support, communications, marketplace, and fraud teams need a shared way to classify and escalate online incidents.

## Authenticity should be easy to verify

Publish official account lists, support channels, seller guidance, and reporting routes. Verification reduces consumer uncertainty and gives internal teams a reliable reference when impersonation appears.

## Response should match the harm

Consumer safety, financial solicitation, credential theft, viral reach, and executive impersonation may require immediate cross-functional action. Lower-risk misuse can enter a standard evidence and enforcement queue.

## Measure customer exposure

Track active audience, engagement, repeat domains, related accounts, customer complaints, and time to containment. Removal count alone does not show whether trust was protected.

Reputation protection is strongest when enforcement and communication reinforce one another: remove supported abuse, help customers recognize authentic channels, and learn from the patterns behind each incident.
