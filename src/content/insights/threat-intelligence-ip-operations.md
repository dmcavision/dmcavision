Traditional enforcement often begins and ends with the reported URL. Threat intelligence asks a broader question: what observable behavior and infrastructure explain how the activity operates and returns?

## Indicators create connection points

Accounts, domains, contact details, destinations, product imagery, descriptions, payment instructions, and posting patterns can link events. Each indicator needs source, timestamp, and confidence.

## Behavior can matter more than identity

Definitively identifying an actor may be difficult. Repeated methods—such as account replacement, traffic redirection, or image reuse—can still guide monitoring and control design.

## Intelligence must remain disciplined

Analytical assessments should distinguish confirmed facts, supported relationships, and unverified hypotheses. Access controls and privacy requirements remain important.

## Outcomes close the loop

Platform action, relisting speed, infrastructure changes, and seller adaptation show whether an assessment was useful.

Threat intelligence does not replace rights analysis. It helps enforcement teams move from isolated removal toward a clearer understanding of persistent online abuse.
