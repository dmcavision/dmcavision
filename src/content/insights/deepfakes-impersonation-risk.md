Synthetic media can imitate executives, creators, employees, or brand representatives with increasing realism. The resulting harm may involve fraud, misinformation, reputation, privacy, publicity rights, trademark use, or platform policy.

## Authenticity signals are contextual

Detection tools can identify manipulation indicators, but compressed or edited media may reduce confidence. Compare the content with official sources, publication context, account history, and distribution behavior.

## Harm determines urgency

Financial requests, credential collection, market-moving claims, safety instructions, and active consumer contact require rapid cross-functional escalation.

## Preserve more than the media file

Capture the post, account, caption, comments, destination links, timestamps, audience signals, and a permitted copy of the media. Context may explain intent and impact.

## Response grounds may overlap

Impersonation, fraud, privacy, trademark, copyright, or synthetic-media policies may each be relevant. Use only supported claims and coordinate legal, security, and communications teams.

Organizations should prepare verification channels and response protocols before a convincing synthetic incident appears.
